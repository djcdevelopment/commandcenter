1 · System map

One boundary, three planes: SENSE observes, ACT does work, and HEARTH is the authenticated/ledgered crossing between callers and both.

Boundary Act Sense Evidence / learning

2 · What a “pour” actually does

HEARTH does not become a second fleet scheduler. It submits through the conductor's existing inbox, preserves the two-builder minimum, and records the crossing.

The assay is a regression gate, not an acceptance oracle. A winner is still a candidate. Required deliverables and behavior need curation before landing; infrastructure failures must not be learned as model-capability failures.

3 · Identity and authority

Authentication says who crossed the door. Authorization says which capability that identity was explicitly granted, and which resource boundary applies.

Normal ingress

Client presents its own key. The key resolves to a caller, the caller names a profile, and the profile grants capabilities.

Temporary GCP ingress

Studio cannot yet send an API-key header, so Funnel → Caddy stamps the scoped gcp-adk-test identity. The URL is effectively the shared secret; no write capability is granted.

Why two ledgers?

The kernel says a call happened and records compact audit facts. The workflow stream says a domain fact happened and retains replayable payloads.

Why the “double write”?

record_event creates a workflow fact and a kernel fact about the call. That is two meanings, not duplicate storage.

5 · The learning loops

“Learning” here is evidence accumulation and policy projection, not hidden online weight updates. Every decision is meant to stay inspectable and reversible.
